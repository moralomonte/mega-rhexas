A transit

A planet can pass between us and its star. For a short while the star looks a tiny bit dimmer. Then it is bright again. That dimming is a transit.

NASA’s TESS mission watches a great many stars and writes down their brightness. Those records are public. Mega Rhexas searches them for transits.

Mega Rhexas reads public NASA TESS data. It is not part of NASA, and NASA has not endorsed it.

What your computer does

The program on your computer does four things, then starts again.

  1. 1. Download

    It downloads a few hundred stars. That is about a gigabyte.

  2. 2. Look

    It looks for a dip that repeats.

  3. 3. Send a tiny result

    The result is much smaller than the star files.

  4. 4. Delete the big files

    After a successful send, the big files go away.

You can close the laptop. The search pauses. When you are back, you can continue.

Why two people check each batch

Every batch of stars goes to two different people. Each one searches it alone.

The batch counts only when both see the same dip. The timing, the strength, and the depth have to match.

If they do not match, neither person gets credit for that try. The stars are checked again.

One person working alone cannot finish a batch. It waits for a second person.

What happens to a candidate

A star with a repeating dip that passes the checks is called a candidate.

Every candidate here is unverified. It is not a confirmed planet. Astronomers still have to study it.

This site does not announce a discovery. A candidate stays unverified until astronomers confirm it.

How much it uses your computer

You choose the pace on your own computer. It does not change anyone else’s search.

  • Eco

    The computer rests between stars. It keeps the batch it already has. It stays quiet so you can keep working.

  • Live

    The usual pace. This is how hard the program runs when you start it.

  • Turbo

    The computer works harder. Other programs may slow down. Use this when you are away from the keyboard.

A batch is about a gigabyte and is deleted after the result is sent. The program stops if the disk is almost full.

Privacy

The big star files stay on your computer until they are deleted. They are not uploaded.

The server receives the small result: the dip measurements for that batch. It does not receive your photos, documents, or other files.

The leaderboard shows the display name you pick. It does not show your email.

There are no coins and no crypto. Credit is a count of stars that two people agreed on.

The beta is invite-only for now.
